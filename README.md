# Watch Brand Map

A plain-English watch buying guide: how to buy a mechanical watch without getting burned, 68 standout picks (35 of them 41mm and up, for larger wrists, 14 hand-wound, 20 with a see-through back, 14 chronographs, 8 travel watches with a second time zone, 7 extra tough ones with hardened cases, 22 whose clasp adjusts by hand and 11 sold with a folding clasp on a strap) with where to buy each one and how to use what it does, working models of the trickier functions (a slide rule, a tachymeter and others) to try on the page, and more than 400 brands rated for fame, value, credibility and character, shown on a map and in a list, with sorting and filters to find the good ones.

It's a single static page with no build step, no tracking and no third-party requests. Fonts and photos are served from this repo. Outbound links to brand and shop sites are plain links.

## Layout

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `site/index.html` | The page: masthead, the how-to-buy guide and the empty containers the script fills. |
| `site/app.js` | All the data (brand tiers, links, the picks with their specs) and the code that draws the cards and the brand index. A watch photo is a button: on a pick it opens a window with a larger photo and the whole card, and on a Main models line it opens the photo with that line's type, sizes, movement, price and description. |
| `site/ratings.js` | One line per brand: four 1–5 ratings (known, value, cred, cool), an approximate count of model lines and an "unusual" flag. All opinion. `app.js` also works out three combined scores out of ten from them and the price range (cost and desirability, cost and value, value and desirability), shown on each row and under the map. "Worth a look" is worked out in `app.js` from value, cred and cool, never from fame. The list and the map open on "Recommended", which is every Worth a look brand plus the brand of each pick. `MICRO` at the end of `ratings.js` lists the brands that count as microbrands, and the Microbrands button shows the ones among them whose cred and cool add up to 7 or more. The Desirable button keeps the brands whose cred and cool add up to 8 or more, whatever their value, and the All brands button (or the Show all brands button under the list) shows the rest. A search looks through every brand, whatever the Show choice, so typing a name always finds it in the list and on the map. |
| `site/reasons.js` | The reasons, in plain sentences. `WHY` has a sentence or two for each recommended brand, keyed by the brand's name in `TIERS`, saying what earns it its ratings; it is shown on the brand's row and in the panel under the map. `PICK_WHY` has a line for each pick, keyed by the pick's `key`, saying why that watch was chosen; it is shown on the pick's card under the description. `HOW` and `PICK_HOW` fill the "How to use it" section on a pick's card: `HOW` explains each kind of function once (stopwatch, tachymeter, slide rule, dive bezel, 12-hour bezel, date and so on) with an everyday example, and `PICK_HOW` lists which of them each pick has, with a note about that watch such as where its sub-dials are. A pick that only tells the time has no entry and no section. An entry in `HOW` with a `demo` gets a "Try it on a working model" link to that model in `lab.js`. Facts in these lines were checked against the makers' sites and the watch press in November 2026. |
| `site/models.js` | `MODELS`: each recommended brand's main model lines (name, type, sizes, movement, typical price, one sentence, a link where found and a `pic` thumbnail where we have one), keyed by the brand's name in `TIERS`. They open from a Main models line under the brand's row and in the panel under the map, and the search box finds brands by model name. Researched in November 2026 from makers' pages, dealers and the watch press. |
| `site/map.js` | The brand map above the index: every brand as a dot, placed by cost, desirability (cred plus cool) and value. There are three views, and each puts two of the three on the axes and shows the third as colour. Picks have a yellow ring, and brands that mostly sell a logo are hollow. It's a hand-drawn SVG chart with no library. It reads the brands and the current filters from `app.js`, so the filters work on the map and the list together, and the list is the same data as a table. Choosing a dot shows that brand under the chart, with a button that finds it in the list. The Hide map button is remembered between visits. |
| `site/lab.js` | The "Try them out" section: six working models drawn in SVG with no library. They are a slide rule (a times table set by turning the outer ring, with six sums each worked in three steps: what you know, set it, read it), a tachymeter (time a car over a kilometre), a telemeter (time lightning to thunder), a stopwatch that can be ordinary or flyback, a second time zone, and dive and countdown bezels. Each can be worked by dragging, by its buttons and sliders, or from the keyboard. It reads nothing from `app.js`. A link to `#lab-<model>` or `#lab-<model>-<mode>` opens that model, which is how the cards link to it. The numbers in its examples match the ones in `HOW`, so change them together. |
| `site/dials.js` | The masthead dial: forty-three designs after well-known watches and clocks, all showing the reader's local time. The newest come first: a new design goes at the top of the list, and a visitor's pinned and seen dials are remembered by name so the order can change. Each page load shows a random one the visitor hasn't seen yet, and the record starts again once all forty-three have been shown. The menu under the dial chooses one for the current visit, Pin keeps it on every visit, and Randomize shows another at random and removes the pin. Clicking the dial steps to the next. Add `?dial=N` to the address to see a particular one. Each design is described once as a list of parts, and the same list feeds the lit renderer and a flat canvas drawing that is used when WebGL is not available. How each part is finished (polished, brushed and in which direction, blasted, lacquer, matte, lume) is set in the design's `fin` field. For testing, `?time=HH:MM:SS` fixes the hands, `?light=x,y` fixes the lamp, `?gl=0` forces the flat drawing and `?debug` exposes `window.__wbmDial`. |
| `site/dial3d.js` | The lit renderer for the dials, hand-written WebGL with no library. It turns a design's parts into height, colour and finish maps, then shades them with a lamp that follows the pointer, set far overhead and wider than the watch so the whole face brightens and dims together (on a phone, the lamp follows how the phone is tilted instead, rests a little to the right for a right thumb, and on iOS starts after a first tap, which asks permission for motion; the dial is full width and centred there), so polished, brushed and matte surfaces catch the light differently. The lists of finishes and of default finishes per part are at the top of the file. |
| `site/releases.js` | The "What's new" notes: one entry per merge to `main`, newest first, and the bell floating at the top left of the dial that opens them in a modal. The bell shows a red dot until the reader has opened the newest entry. |
| `site/style.css` | The design: one typeface (Jost), a ruled grid, and blue, red and yellow used as signals. |
| `site/nav.js` | The floating bar. On a window 1200 pixels wide or more it is a rail down the left edge, as tall as the window, listing the sections; the section being read lists the places inside it (guide boxes, picks, working models, price tiers and brands) and that list scrolls inside the rail. Once the first screen has scrolled away the rail also has search and filters for the brand list. On a narrower window it is a bar along the bottom that opens the same rail as a drawer. It drives the brand list through the list's own controls, so `app.js` doesn't know about it. |
| `site/full.js` | `FULL`: the photos that also have a larger copy in `site/img/full/` under the same file name, with its pixel width. The inspector in `app.js` (click a photo in a photo window, or a brand's photo) loads that copy so the photo can be enlarged and dragged about, and links to the file. |
| `site/img/` | Photos of the picks, one `b-*.jpg` photo per brand in the index (up to 600 pixels square; 158 are still the 176 pixel originals), and one `m-<brand>-<model>.jpg` photo (up to 600 pixels square, on white; 26 are still 240) for each main model line that has a photo. `style.css` sets how large the brand and model photos show at each screen width through `--ph` and `--mph`: big enough to see the watch without opening it on a phone, and at least three inches on a 27 inch 4K monitor. |
| `site/fonts/` | Jost, served from this repo. |
| `research/dial-finishes/` | The research behind the dial finishes: one `design-NN.json` per dial, numbered in the order the first thirty-one were made (the menu now lists newest first, and the twelve newer dials have no research file yet). Each names the real watch or clock it follows and lists every surface seen from the front with its material, its finish, the direction of any brushing, a confidence level and a source. It also notes how the piece catches light and which mistakes to avoid. It is not part of the published site. `site/dials.js` holds the finishes actually used, in each design's `fin` field. |
| `.github/workflows/pages.yml` | Deploys `site/` to GitHub Pages on every push to `main`. |

## Updating

Edit the files in `site/` directly, then commit and push. Every brand in `TIERS` needs a line in `ratings.js` under the same name. A brand that becomes recommended (Worth a look, or the brand of a pick) needs a line in `WHY` in `reasons.js`, and a new pick needs a line in `PICK_WHY`, plus a line in `PICK_HOW` if it does anything beyond telling the time. A pick with a chip containing "Chronograph" is shown by the Chronograph filter. A pick with a chip containing "GMT" is shown by the Second time zone filter. Call it "GMT travel watch" when the ordinary hour hand jumps on its own and give it the `gmt` line in `PICK_HOW`; call it "Caller GMT" when only the 24-hour hand sets on its own and give it `gmtcaller`, since the working model shows the first kind. A 12-hour bezel does not count. The Extra tough filter shows the picks tagged `tough`: only tag a watch whose maker (or its official dealer) states a hardened case with a hardness figure, and put what is hardened and the figure in `specs.hard`, which is shown as the Hardening line on the card. Every pick also has bracelet and strap lines in `specs`: `lug` (strap width), `band` (the bracelet), `adjust` (whether the length changes by hand, and by how much) and `strap` (the strap and its buckle). The Adjusts by hand filter shows the picks tagged `easy`: only tag a watch when the maker, its dealer or a hands-on review says the clasp moves in small steps without tools, and start its `adjust` line with "Yes". A quick-release bracelet or a fold-out diver's extension does not count. The Folding-clasp strap filter shows the picks tagged `fold`: the maker sells the watch on a strap with a folding clasp, or lists a folding clasp for that model as an extra, and the `strap` line starts with "Folding clasp". A clasp the maker only sells for its straps in general can be mentioned in the line but does not earn the tag. Where the maker says nothing, write "None listed", not "No". The notes under the filter buttons come from `PF_NOTE` in `app.js`. A pick's `brandKey` is the name of its brand in `TIERS`; where the two are spelled differently, add the pair to `PICK_ROW` in `app.js`. The map needs no upkeep: it draws whatever is in the index. To add a brand thumbnail, put a square JPEG in `site/img/` and set `"img"` on that brand's entry in `LINKS` in `app.js`. To add a model photo, put a square JPEG in `site/img/` and set `"pic"` on that line in `MODELS`; a line without one is shown without a thumbnail. Each pick's `specs` object holds its movement, case, water resistance and crystal, the bracelet and strap lines, plus the hardening on the extra tough picks.

Every pull request that changes what readers see adds an entry at the top of `RELEASES` in `site/releases.js`: the pull request number as `id`, the merge date, a short title, and optional notes.

Preview locally with `python3 -m http.server -d site 8000`.

## Security

- Content-Security-Policy is set in a `<meta>` tag (GitHub Pages can't send custom headers): scripts, styles, images and fonts only from this site, no forms, no outbound connections.
- The workflow runs only on pushes to `main`, uses minimal token permissions per job, and pins every action to a commit SHA.
