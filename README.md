# Watch Brand Map

A plain-English watch buying guide: how to buy a mechanical watch without getting burned, 68 standout picks (35 of them 41mm and up, for larger wrists, 14 hand-wound, 20 with a see-through back, 14 chronographs, 8 travel watches with a second time zone, 7 extra tough ones with hardened cases, 22 whose clasp adjusts by hand and 11 sold with a folding clasp on a strap) with where to buy each one and how to use what it does, working models of the trickier functions (a slide rule, a tachymeter and others) to try on the page, and more than 400 brands rated for fame, value, credibility and character, shown on a map and in a list, with sorting and filters to find the good ones.

It's a single static page with no build step, no tracking and no third-party requests. Fonts and photos are served from this repo. Outbound links to brand and shop sites are plain links.

## Layout

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `site/index.html` | The page: masthead, the how-to-buy guide and the empty containers the script fills. |
| `site/app.js` | All the data (brand tiers, links, the picks with their specs) and the code that draws the cards and the brand index. A watch photo is a button: on a pick it opens a window with a larger photo and the whole card, and on a Main models line it opens the photo with that line's type, sizes, movement, price and description. |
| `site/ratings.js` | One line per brand: four 1–5 ratings (known, value, cred, cool), an approximate count of model lines and an "unusual" flag. All opinion. `app.js` also works out three combined scores out of ten from them and the price range (cost and desirability, cost and value, value and desirability), shown on each row and under the map. "Worth a look" is worked out in `app.js` from value, cred and cool, never from fame. The list and the map open on "Recommended", which is every Worth a look brand plus the brand of each pick. `MICRO` at the end of `ratings.js` lists the brands that count as microbrands, and the Microbrands button shows the ones among them whose cred and cool add up to 7 or more. The Desirable button keeps the brands whose cred and cool add up to 8 or more, whatever their value, and the All brands button (or the Show all brands button under the list) shows the rest. A search looks through every brand, whatever the Show choice, so typing a name always finds it in the list and on the map. |
| `site/reasons.js` | The reasons, in plain sentences. `WHY` has a sentence or two for each recommended brand, keyed by the brand's name in `TIERS`, saying what earns it its ratings; it is shown on the brand's row and in the panel under the map. `PICK_WHY` has a line for each pick, keyed by the pick's `key`, saying why that watch was chosen; it is shown on the pick's card under the description. `HOW` and `PICK_HOW` fill the "How to use it" section on a pick's card: `HOW` explains each kind of function once (stopwatch, tachymeter, slide rule, dive bezel, 12-hour bezel, date and so on) with an everyday example, and `PICK_HOW` lists which of them each pick has, with a note about that watch such as where its sub-dials are. A pick that only tells the time has no entry and no section. An entry in `HOW` with a `demo` gets a "Try it on a working model" link to that model in `lab.js`. Facts in these lines were checked against the makers' sites and the watch press in November 2026. |
| `site/models.js` | `MODELS`: each recommended brand's main model lines (name, type, sizes, movement, typical price, one sentence, a link where found and a `pic` thumbnail where we have one), keyed by the brand's name in `TIERS`. They open from a Main models line under the brand's row and in the panel under the map, and the search box finds brands by model name. Researched in November 2026 from makers' pages, dealers and the watch press. |
| `site/map.js` | The brand map above the index: every brand as a dot, placed by cost, desirability (cred plus cool) and value. It's a hand-drawn SVG chart with no library. It reads the brands and the current filters from `app.js`, so the filters work on the map and the list together, and the list is the same data as a table. Choosing a dot shows that brand under the chart, with a button that finds it in the list. The Hide map button is remembered between visits. |
| `site/lab.js` | The "Try them out" section: six working models drawn in SVG with no library. They are a slide rule (a times table set by turning the outer ring, with six sums each worked in three steps: what you know, set it, read it), a tachymeter (time a car over a kilometre), a telemeter (time lightning to thunder), a stopwatch that can be ordinary or flyback, a second time zone, and dive and countdown bezels. Each can be worked by dragging, by its buttons and sliders, or from the keyboard. It reads nothing from `app.js`. A link to `#lab-<model>` or `#lab-<model>-<mode>` opens that model, which is how the cards link to it. The numbers in its examples match the ones in `HOW`, so change them together. |
| `site/dials.js` | The masthead dial: forty-three designs after well-known watches and clocks, all showing the reader's local time. The newest come first: a new design goes at the top of the list, and a visitor's pinned and seen dials are remembered by name so the order can change. Each page load shows a random one the visitor hasn't seen yet, and the record starts again once all forty-three have been shown. The menu under the dial chooses one for the current visit, Pin keeps it on every visit, and Randomize shows another at random and removes the pin. Clicking the dial steps to the next. `?dial=` in the address shows a particular one for that load, without changing the pin: a slug of its name (`?dial=panda-chronograph`, lower case, accents and apostrophes dropped, hyphens between words) or `N` for the Nth in the list as it stands. Each design is described once as a list of parts, and the same list feeds the lit renderer and a flat canvas drawing that is used when WebGL is not available. How each part is finished (polished, brushed and in which direction, blasted, lacquer, matte, lume) is set in the design's `fin` field. For testing, `?time=HH:MM:SS` fixes the hands, `?light=x,y` fixes the lamp, `?gl=0` forces the flat drawing and `?debug` exposes `window.__wbmDial`. |

It turns a design's parts into height, colour and finish maps, then shades them with a lamp that follows the pointer, set far overhead and wider than the watch so the whole face brightens and dims together (on a phone, the lamp follows how the phone is tilted instead, rests a little to the right for a right thumb, and on iOS starts after a first tap, which asks permission for motion; the dial is full width and centred there), so polished, brushed and matte surfaces catch the light differently. The lists of finishes and of default finishes per part are at the top of the file. |

Choosing a match goes there and marks it for a moment once the page has stopped scrolling. The bar's search box and the brand list's own search box are separate. |
| `site/find.js` | `FIND`, the matcher behind the bar's search. It knows nothing about the page. Case and accents are ignored, what was typed is split into words, and every word has to match an entry's name or its secondary text (a pick's chips, a brand's country and standout watch, a model line's brand), with the name counting for more. A typed word matches, best first, a whole word, the start of one, words run together ("blackbay"), the inside of one, a whole word with one or two typos ("navitmer", "seko" for Seiko, "divers" for diver), its letters in order with gaps ("blk" for "black"), or the start of a longer word with a typo. The last two kinds are the loose ones: when anything matches a whole word, the start of one or words run together, entries that match only loosely are left out, so "try" finds Try them out and not Tourby. A typo of a whole word is never left out. The scores are in `word`, `best` and `match`. |

It also notes how the piece catches light and which mistakes to avoid. It is not part of the published site. `site/dials.js` holds the finishes actually used, in each design's `fin` field. |

## The 3D watch

The Full watch button under the masthead dial swaps the flat dial for a 3D watch the reader can turn: a blue and cream slide-rule chronograph on a steel bracelet, after the Breitling Navitimer. `site/watch3d.js` owns the button, the view chooser that takes the place of the bracelet menu, and when the model's frame loop runs (only while the watch is showing, on screen, in a visible tab). A table in it, `WATCHES`, names the faces that have a 3D watch and the watch each one opens; for now there is one row, the Slide-rule chronograph and the Navitimer. Pressing the button on a face in the table opens its watch; on any other face it first switches the masthead to the Slide-rule chronograph dial (`DEFAULT`) through `window.WBMDials` in `dials.js`. Choosing another face in the table while the mode is on shows that face's watch in place of the one showing; choosing a face that is not in it leaves the mode. One watch is held at a time. The mode is in the address too, kept up to date with `replaceState`: `?full=1` opens the 3D watch on arrival as a press of the button would, and `?view=front|side|back|clasp|bracelet` picks its view (the three-quarter view, and a watch turned by hand, have no `view`). For close-ups, `?cam=az,el,dist` or `?cam=az,el,dist,x,y,z` with `?full=1` places the camera anywhere instead: degrees round from the front towards 3 o'clock, degrees up towards 12 o'clock (between -90 and 90), millimetres from the point looked at (30 to 520, the reader's zoom limits), and that point in the watch's millimetres (0,0,0 if left out); for example `?full=1&cam=70,10,60,22,0,6` looks at the crown. It is read on arrival only, the view list then reads Turned by hand, it is dropped from the address at the page's next change to it and never written back, and a malformed or out-of-range one is ignored, leaving the view as it was. `?time=HH:MM:SS` freezes the clock of the flat dial and the 3D watch alike, and `?date=YYYY-MM-DD` with it sets the date they show (today's without it; a date that does not exist, or one without a good `time=`, is ignored); both are in the machine's own time zone. Each time a watch is shown, `site/watch3d.js` writes its report as JSON into the stage's `data-watch3d-report` attribute, always rather than only under `?debug`, so a check can load exactly the address a reader would. `site/watch3d/` holds the models. `kit/` is shared by every watch and names none: `stage.js` (`mount(container, { watch, ... })` puts a watch in an element and returns the handle the button drives, with `setView`, `setCamera` (the `cam` form above), `start`, `stop`, `dispose`, `about`, `report` and `show(id)` to show another watch on the same renderer, freeing the one before but not the kit's own materials, textures and studio), `materials.js` (steel, rhodium, lume, glass and the brushed grain), `env.js` (the studio the steel reflects), `geo.js` (shape tools), `type.js` (lettering tools) and `loop.js` (the solver that drapes a bracelet in a closed loop, given its dimensions). `watches/<id>/index.js` exports `about` (the id, the canvas's label, the hint shown after the caption, all strings, and optionally a caption, its own placing of any of the six views, as it cannot add a view, and `light`) and `build`, which returns the watch's group, a `tick` that moves its hands, the parts the reader can press, and a `report`: whatever the watch wants to record plus `warnings`, a list of lines for its own checks that failed. A watch must put every check of its own that fails there, not only in the console; `reportLoop(report, 'bracelet', checkLoop(L))` from `kit/loop.js` does it for a bracelet loop (and prints them with `console.warn` too), as the Navitimer does. A report that is not plain data, or whose `warnings` is not a list, gets a warning saying so rather than vanishing. `build` is given `{ M, renderer, frozen }`. `M` is the build's own copies of the kit's materials, made afresh for each build and freed with the watch, so a watch may change them without touching the next watch; the copies share the kit's textures, which a watch must not change (clone one to change it). `renderer` is shared: a watch should leave it alone, and whatever it changes of the renderer's, scene's and camera's settings (tone mapping and exposure, colour space, the shadow map, clipping, clear colour, pixel ratio, the scene's environment, background and fog, the camera's field of view and range, and the rest listed in the contract in `kit/stage.js`) is put back when the watch is freed. `frozen` is true when the page's `?time=` pins the clock, as in the shots: the watch must then show the time `tick` is given and move nothing by `dt` (a balance, a rotor, a sweep or stopwatch counted frame by frame), because the shots are taken in real time after a varying number of frames and anything that moves shows up as noise. Each view in `about.views` is `{ dir, dist, target }`: the direction from the target to the camera (three numbers, not all zero), the distance (30 to 520 mm) and the point looked at (three numbers); each part in `press` is `{ object, down, up? }` with `object` in the watch's group. A view or press entry not of that form makes the watch fail to show, with the reason in the console. The stage hands the report out as `{ watch, triangles, drawCalls, geometries, textures, warnings, ...the watch's own entries }`, the counts being the renderer's for the first frame in the three-quarter view. `about.light` lets a watch adjust the kit's light for itself and no further: `{ key, rim, back: { strength, dir }, exposure, environment }`, every field optional, where `strength`, `exposure` and `environment` multiply the kit's values (0 to 4) and `dir` turns a lamp about the point it aims at; the studio, colours, shadows and tone mapping stay the kit's. The stage applies it when the watch is shown and restores its own light when the watch is freed, and rejects a malformed one. The Navitimer sets no `light`; `watches/navitimer/` is the Navitimer, whose other modules build the case, dial, hands, case back and bracelet in code. Adding a watch is a folder in `watches/` and a row in `WATCHES`.

Nothing under `site/watch3d/` is requested until the button is first pressed, so a reader who never presses it downloads none of it. The model is loaded with a dynamic `import()` and every import inside it is a relative path, so the page needs no import map and the Content-Security-Policy is unchanged. three.js r187 (MIT, licence in `site/watch3d/vendor/LICENSE-three.txt`) is vendored there with its OrbitControls and BufferGeometryUtils add-ons. `three.module.js` and `three.core.js` are the published files unchanged; the two add-ons (`OrbitControls.js`, `BufferGeometryUtils.js`) differ from the published files only in their import line, which names `./three.module.js` instead of the bare `three`. It is the unminified build, about 2.2 MB before compression, because the npm package of that version has no minified one.

To check that a change leaves a watch looking the same, serve the repository, shoot its six views before and after with `tools/watch3d-shots.sh [--allow-warnings] [--no-report] [--scale N] http://127.0.0.1:<port>/site/ <dial-slug> <dir> [name=cam ...]`, and compare the two sets with `tools/watch3d-compare.py <dir-a> <dir-b> [--limits FILE]`. The page's `?time=10:08:30&date=2026-11-26` pins the clock, and Chromium runs in the Asia/Tokyo time zone, which is not UTC, so a 24-hour hand stands apart from the hour hand, and has no summer time, so the same pictures come out on any day and machine. The script takes a picture only once the 3D watch is on screen, deletes an old one first, stops a Chromium run after 90 seconds, and fails a view at once, with the page's last console line as the reason, when the page gives up (the build threw, or there is no WebGL). It draws on the graphics card unless `WATCH3D_RENDERER=software` asks for Chromium's software renderer, and says which it used; compare only sets shot with the same renderer and scale. For each picture it prints, and saves beside it as `<view>.json`, the WebGL renderer string, the watch's report, console errors and warnings, page errors and failed requests, on a failure too. It exits with an error when a view had a console error, a page error, a failed request under the site, no report, a watch that left the screen in the 2 seconds after its picture (its `tick` threw, say), a warning in the report, or a console warning; `--allow-warnings` lets the last two kinds through, and `--no-report` lets a page with no report through, for shooting a commit from before the page wrote one (417bc3c and earlier). A watch that prints a failed check to the console without putting it in its report therefore still fails. `--scale 2` shoots at twice the pixels (2880 by 1800: the masthead's canvas is then 800 pixels wide and the Navitimer's head 467 pixels across; the stage draws at no more than 2), and each `name=cam` argument shoots one more picture, `<name>.png`, from that `?cam=`, for example `crown=70,10,60,22,0,6`; a cam the page ignores fails the run. The compare script compares the six views and every other picture in either folder (a close-up missing from one set fails), and refuses to compare sets shot with different renderers. Two shots of the same code are not quite identical, so it holds limits for each view and each renderer (the largest channel difference and the number of differing pixels allowed, counted at 1440 pixels wide and scaled by the square of the width for other scales), set a little above the worst seen between sets of the Navitimer shot from the same code: on the graphics card, twelve sets shot before the 26 November driver update, 10 levels and 120 pixels for the hero, front and bracelet views, 16 and 120 for the side, 150 and 220 for the clasp and 180 and 1,800 for the back; in software (SwiftShader), fifteen sets, 10 and 120 for the hero, front and bracelet, 100 and 180 for the side, 220 and 900 for the clasp and 255 and 4,500 for the back. A close-up gets 10 and 120. In software the clasp view's noise changed with how busy the machine was, and the graphics card's sets were all shot in one sitting, so its back and clasp limits may be too tight: if only those views go over, shoot again before blaming the change. `--max-diff N` and `--max-pixels N` replace a limit for every picture. It also prints how many pixels differ by more than 8 levels: the smoothing of text and edges stays below that, so a count there usually means a real change. The noise in the back, clasp and side views lies round the case back's window. Its cause is not fully known: the balance and rotor behind the window move on the frame loop's own clock, but a review saw the back view differ by about 1,500 pixels between two runs with the frame loop never started.

These limits are the Navitimer's. Before relying on them for a new watch, shoot it twice from the same code (same renderer, same scale), compare the two sets with `--max-diff 255 --max-pixels 1000000` to see its own noise, and if any view is noisier than the Navitimer's limits write the watch's own in a small JSON file given with `--limits FILE`: `{ "renderer": "software", "back": [255, 6000], "crown": [10, 200], "*": [10, 120] }`, each entry `[largest difference, differing pixels at 1440 wide]` for a view or close-up by name, `"*"` for any picture not named, and names left out keeping the built-in limits. A watch whose picture moves while frozen will show it here.

The checking tools, all in `tools/`, plain node or Python with no packages, each with its full usage in its header (the browser tools take `WATCH3D_RENDERER=software` like the shots script):

- `watch3d-shots.sh [--allow-warnings] [--no-report] [--scale N] <base-url> <dial-slug> <out-dir> [name=cam ...]`: the six views and any close-ups, with the report and console of each, as above. It drives `watch3d-shot.mjs [--no-report] [--allow-warnings] <png> <json> <site-url> <seconds> <page-url> <chrome> [chrome flags...]`, one picture per Chromium run.
- `watch3d-compare.py <dir-a> <dir-b> [--limits FILE] [--max-diff N] [--max-pixels N]`: compares two sets, as above.
- `node tools/watch3d-loopcheck.mjs <worktree> [watch-folder] [key=value...]`: runs a watch's bracelet build in node, with no browser, and prints the loop's figures and failures (pin spacing, the seam, joints that bend too far or backwards, a pin inside the case, an end link wider than the lugs); exits 1 on any failure. `key=value` changes one figure of `BRACELET` for a trial that must fail. It needs `site/watch3d/watches/<watch>/bracelet.js` to load in node (imports by relative path, no canvas or DOM while building) and to export `buildBracelet({ M, report })`, which fills `report.bracelet = { failures, pin0, seam, pins, links, length, ... }` with the loop it really builds, and `BRACELET`, the written figures. Without a `buildBracelet` that runs, it solves the loop from `BRACELET` alone and still ends FAIL, unless `WRITTEN_ONLY=1` is given for a plan that has no builder yet.
- `node tools/watch3d-console.mjs <page-url> [seconds-to-listen]`: loads a page and prints the renderer, a lost context, and every console error and warning, uncaught error and failed request; exits 0 only when the watch showed and nothing was reported.
- `node tools/watch3d-closeup.mjs <page-url> <out-dir> <shot>...`: pictures from the set views, a view turned by a few degrees, or (on a page with a handle) any camera.
- `node tools/watch3d-lamps.mjs <page-url> [out-dir]`: scales the lamps and measures the brightness of the steel and the dial at each strength; it needs a page with a scene handle (the lab page).
- `node tools/watch3d-measure.mjs mean|diff|span ...`: the mean brightness of a box, how much two pictures differ, or how wide a part is along a row or column, so a figure quoted from a shot can be worked out again.
- `watch3d-browser.mjs`: shared by the `.mjs` browser tools, not run by itself.

The model was built by an agent as a trial and copied from `lab/fable-navitimer/` on the branch `sporaktu/fable-navitimer` at commit `00d5fc2`; its `NOTES.md` there describes it in full. Changed in the copy: bare imports became relative ones, the full-window page became `mount(container, options)` with `start`, `stop`, `setView` and `dispose`, the dark backdrop was dropped so the watch sits on the page's own ground (and the crystal's blending was changed so that clear glass stays clear over the page), the mouse wheel was left to the page so it still scrolls, and options took the place of the page-address parameters. The material test bench and the lab page were not copied, and the font is the site's own. The lettering is as the agent made it. The copy will drift from the lab one; nothing keeps them in step.

Known simplifications, from the model's notes:

- The bezel and the crown do not turn, the bracelet is fixed in one closed loop and the clasp does not open.
- The movement behind the case back is invented to look the part, the crystal does not bend the view of the dial, and the lume does not glow.
- Colours, bracelet details and case proportions are estimates from pictures, and the polished bracelet rows can turn nearly black from some angles.

## Updating

Edit the files in `site/` directly, then commit and push. Every brand in `TIERS` needs a line in `ratings.js` under the same name. A brand that becomes recommended (Worth a look, or the brand of a pick) needs a line in `WHY` in `reasons.js`, and a new pick needs a line in `PICK_WHY`, plus a line in `PICK_HOW` if it does anything beyond telling the time. A pick with a chip containing "Chronograph" is shown by the Chronograph filter. A pick with a chip containing "GMT" is shown by the Second time zone filter. Call it "GMT travel watch" when the ordinary hour hand jumps on its own and give it the `gmt` line in `PICK_HOW`; call it "Caller GMT" when only the 24-hour hand sets on its own and give it `gmtcaller`, since the working model shows the first kind. A 12-hour bezel does not count. The Extra tough filter shows the picks tagged `tough`: only tag a watch whose maker (or its official dealer) states a hardened case with a hardness figure, and put what is hardened and the figure in `specs.hard`, which is shown as the Hardening line on the card. Every pick also has bracelet and strap lines in `specs`: `lug` (strap width), `band` (the bracelet), `adjust` (whether the length changes by hand, and by how much) and `strap` (the strap and its buckle). The Adjusts by hand filter shows the picks tagged `easy`: only tag a watch when the maker, its dealer or a hands-on review says the clasp moves in small steps without tools, and start its `adjust` line with "Yes". A quick-release bracelet or a fold-out diver's extension does not count. The Folding-clasp strap filter shows the picks tagged `fold`: the maker sells the watch on a strap with a folding clasp, or lists a folding clasp for that model as an extra, and the `strap` line starts with "Folding clasp". A clasp the maker only sells for its straps in general can be mentioned in the line but does not earn the tag. Where the maker says nothing, write "None listed", not "No". The notes under the filter buttons come from `PF_NOTE` in `app.js`. A pick's `brandKey` is the name of its brand in `TIERS`; where the two are spelled differently, add the pair to `PICK_ROW` in `app.js`. The map needs no upkeep: it draws whatever is in the index. To add a brand thumbnail, put a square JPEG in `site/img/` and set `"img"` on that brand's entry in `LINKS` in `app.js`. To add a model photo, put a square JPEG in `site/img/` and set `"pic"` on that line in `MODELS`; a line without one is shown without a thumbnail. Each pick's `specs` object holds its movement, case, water resistance and crystal, the bracelet and strap lines, plus the hardening on the extra tough picks.

Every pull request that changes what readers see adds an entry at the top of `RELEASES` in `site/releases.js`: the pull request number as `id`, the merge date, a short title, and optional notes.

Preview locally with `python3 -m http.server -d site 8000`.

## Security

- Content-Security-Policy is set in a `<meta>` tag (GitHub Pages can't send custom headers): scripts, styles, images and fonts only from this site, no forms, no outbound connections.
- The workflow runs only on pushes to `main`, uses minimal token permissions per job, and pins every action to a commit SHA.
