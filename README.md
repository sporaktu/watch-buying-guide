# Watch Brand Map

A plain-English watch buying guide: how to buy a mechanical watch without getting burned, 52 standout picks (25 of them 41mm and up, for larger wrists, 11 chronographs, 7 extra tough ones with hardened cases, 18 whose clasp adjusts by hand and 10 sold with a folding clasp on a strap) with where to buy each one and how to use what it does, working models of the trickier functions (a slide rule, a tachymeter and others) to try on the page, and more than 400 brands rated for fame, value, credibility and character, shown on a map and in a list, with sorting and filters to find the good ones.

It's a single static page with no build step, no tracking and no third-party requests. Fonts and photos are served from this repo. Outbound links to brand and shop sites are plain links.

## Layout

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `site/index.html` | The page: masthead, the how-to-buy guide and the empty containers the script fills. |
| `site/app.js` | All the data (brand tiers, links, the picks with their specs) and the code that draws the cards and the brand index. |
| `site/ratings.js` | One line per brand: four 1–5 ratings (known, value, cred, cool), an approximate count of model lines and an "unusual" flag. All opinion. "Worth a look" is worked out in `app.js` from value, cred and cool, never from fame. The list and the map open on "Recommended", which is every Worth a look brand plus the brand of each pick. The Desirable button keeps the brands whose cred and cool add up to 8 or more, whatever their value, and the All brands button (or the Show all brands button under the list) shows the rest. A search looks through every brand, whatever the Show choice, so typing a name always finds it in the list and on the map. |
| `site/reasons.js` | The reasons, in plain sentences. `WHY` has a sentence or two for each recommended brand, keyed by the brand's name in `TIERS`, saying what earns it its ratings; it is shown on the brand's row and in the panel under the map. `PICK_WHY` has a line for each pick, keyed by the pick's `key`, saying why that watch was chosen; it is shown on the pick's card under the description. `HOW` and `PICK_HOW` fill the "How to use it" section on a pick's card: `HOW` explains each kind of function once (stopwatch, tachymeter, slide rule, dive bezel, 12-hour bezel, date and so on) with an everyday example, and `PICK_HOW` lists which of them each pick has, with a note about that watch such as where its sub-dials are. A pick that only tells the time has no entry and no section. An entry in `HOW` with a `demo` gets a "Try it on a working model" link to that model in `lab.js`. Facts in these lines were checked against the makers' sites and the watch press in November 2026. |
| `site/map.js` | The brand map above the index: every brand as a dot, placed by cost, desirability (cred plus cool) and value. There are three views, and each puts two of the three on the axes and shows the third as colour. Picks have a yellow ring, and brands that mostly sell a logo are hollow. It's a hand-drawn SVG chart with no library. It reads the brands and the current filters from `app.js`, so the filters work on the map and the list together, and the list is the same data as a table. Choosing a dot shows that brand under the chart, with a button that finds it in the list. The Hide map button is remembered between visits. |
| `site/lab.js` | The "Try them out" section: six working models drawn in SVG with no library. They are a slide rule (a times table set by turning the outer ring, with six sums each worked in three steps: what you know, set it, read it), a tachymeter (time a car over a kilometre), a telemeter (time lightning to thunder), a stopwatch that can be ordinary or flyback, a second time zone, and dive and countdown bezels. Each can be worked by dragging, by its buttons and sliders, or from the keyboard. It reads nothing from `app.js`. A link to `#lab-<model>` or `#lab-<model>-<mode>` opens that model, which is how the cards link to it. The numbers in its examples match the ones in `HOW`, so change them together. |
| `site/dials.js` | The masthead dial: thirty-one designs after well-known watches and clocks, all showing the reader's local time. Each page load shows a random one the visitor hasn't seen yet, and the record starts again once all thirty-one have been shown. The menu under the dial chooses one for the current visit, Pin keeps it on every visit, and Randomize shows another at random and removes the pin. Clicking the dial steps to the next. Add `?dial=N` to the address to see a particular one. Each design is described once as a list of parts, and the same list feeds the lit renderer and a flat canvas drawing that is used when WebGL is not available. How each part is finished (polished, brushed and in which direction, blasted, lacquer, matte, lume) is set in the design's `fin` field. For testing, `?time=HH:MM:SS` fixes the hands, `?light=x,y` fixes the lamp, `?gl=0` forces the flat drawing and `?debug` exposes `window.__wbmDial`. |
| `site/dial3d.js` | The lit renderer for the dials, hand-written WebGL with no library. It turns a design's parts into height, colour and finish maps, then shades them with a lamp that follows the pointer (on a phone, the lamp follows how the phone is tilted instead, rests a little to the right for a right thumb, and on iOS starts after a first tap, which asks permission for motion; the dial is full width and centred there), so polished, brushed and matte surfaces catch the light differently. The lists of finishes and of default finishes per part are at the top of the file. |
| `site/style.css` | The design: one typeface (Jost), a ruled grid, and blue, red and yellow used as signals. |
| `site/img/` | Photos of the picks, plus one small `b-*.jpg` thumbnail per brand in the index. |
| `site/fonts/` | Jost, served from this repo. |
| `research/dial-finishes/` | The research behind the dial finishes: one `design-NN.json` per dial, numbered as in the dial menu. Each names the real watch or clock it follows and lists every surface seen from the front with its material, its finish, the direction of any brushing, a confidence level and a source. It also notes how the piece catches light and which mistakes to avoid. It is not part of the published site. `site/dials.js` holds the finishes actually used, in each design's `fin` field. |
| `.github/workflows/pages.yml` | Deploys `site/` to GitHub Pages on every push to `main`. |

## Updating

Edit the files in `site/` directly, then commit and push. Every brand in `TIERS` needs a line in `ratings.js` under the same name. A brand that becomes recommended (Worth a look, or the brand of a pick) needs a line in `WHY` in `reasons.js`, and a new pick needs a line in `PICK_WHY`, plus a line in `PICK_HOW` if it does anything beyond telling the time. A pick with a "Chronograph" chip is shown by the Chronograph filter. The Extra tough filter shows the picks tagged `tough`: only tag a watch whose maker (or its official dealer) states a hardened case with a hardness figure, and put what is hardened and the figure in `specs.hard`, which is shown as the Hardening line on the card. Every pick also has bracelet and strap lines in `specs`: `lug` (strap width), `band` (the bracelet), `adjust` (whether the length changes by hand, and by how much) and `strap` (the strap and its buckle). The Adjusts by hand filter shows the picks tagged `easy`: only tag a watch when the maker, its dealer or a hands-on review says the clasp moves in small steps without tools, and start its `adjust` line with "Yes". A quick-release bracelet or a fold-out diver's extension does not count. The Folding-clasp strap filter shows the picks tagged `fold`: the maker sells the watch on a strap with a folding clasp, or lists a folding clasp for that model as an extra, and the `strap` line starts with "Folding clasp". A clasp the maker only sells for its straps in general can be mentioned in the line but does not earn the tag. Where the maker says nothing, write "None listed", not "No". The notes under the filter buttons come from `PF_NOTE` in `app.js`. A pick's `brandKey` is the name of its brand in `TIERS`; where the two are spelled differently, add the pair to `PICK_ROW` in `app.js`. The map needs no upkeep: it draws whatever is in the index. To add a brand thumbnail, put a square JPEG in `site/img/` and set `"img"` on that brand's entry in `LINKS` in `app.js`. Each pick's `specs` object holds its movement, case, water resistance and crystal, the bracelet and strap lines, plus the hardening on the extra tough picks.

Preview locally with `python3 -m http.server -d site 8000`.

## Security

- Content-Security-Policy is set in a `<meta>` tag (GitHub Pages can't send custom headers): scripts, styles, images and fonts only from this site, no forms, no outbound connections.
- The workflow runs only on pushes to `main`, uses minimal token permissions per job, and pins every action to a commit SHA.
